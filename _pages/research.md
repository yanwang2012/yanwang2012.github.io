Our research connects compact-object astrophysics, gravitational-wave source
modeling, and statistical inference. We focus on the nanohertz band observed
with pulsar timing arrays and the millihertz band targeted by space-based
interferometers, alongside broader questions in relativistic astrophysics and
gravity.

## PTA Data Analysis

Pulsar timing arrays use precise observations of millisecond pulsars to search
for nanohertz gravitational waves. We develop detection and parameter-estimation
methods for individual supermassive black hole binaries, including continuous
signals and ringdown signals. Our work addresses pulsar-term uncertainties,
multiple-source resolution, and red-noise mitigation, with an emphasis on
scalable analyses for large pulsar arrays. Related studies examine stochastic
backgrounds and their anisotropy.

Representative papers:

- [Iterative time-domain resolution of multiple PTA sources (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.106.023016)
- [Scalable continuous-wave detection with red-noise suppression and pulsar selection (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1103/1yqv-39pd)

## Space-Based Gravitational-Wave Data Analysis

We develop data-analysis methods for space-based observatories such as LISA
and TianQin. Our targets include stellar-mass and massive black hole binaries
and extreme-mass-ratio inspirals. Research topics range from detector response,
orbital modeling, and time-delay interferometry to hierarchical signal searches
and parameter estimation. We also investigate environmental noise and the
impact of unresolved Galactic binaries on stochastic-background inference.
These studies connect instrument-level modeling with astrophysical analysis.

Representative papers:

- [Hierarchical searches for stellar-mass binary black holes in noisy space-based detector data (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.111.043026)
- [An analysis pipeline for extremely large-mass-ratio inspirals (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1103/3hx6-7zyb)
- [Time-delay interferometry and principal component analysis for TianQin (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1674-1056/ae12d4)

## Gravitational-Wave Astrophysics

We study the information carried by gravitational waves from binaries
containing black holes, neutron stars, and white dwarfs. Our work includes
multiband and multimessenger observations of Galactic double neutron stars,
the detectability and populations of double white dwarfs, and continuous-wave
emission from spinning neutron stars in binaries. Waveform studies of spin
coupling and orbital eccentricity help connect the observed signals to binary
dynamics and compact-object properties.

Representative papers:

- [Inferring double-neutron-star binary parameters with dual-line gravitational-wave detection (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.109.043033)
- [Detecting eclipsing double white dwarfs with electromagnetic and gravitational waves (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae2696)

## Relativistic Astrophysics and Gravity

Our broader research explores how strong gravity affects astrophysical
observables. Topics include relativistic effects on pulsar arrival times,
emission-line profiles from warped accretion disks around Kerr black holes,
and compact-binary gravitational waveforms in alternative theories of gravity.
These studies provide a theoretical basis for interpreting precision
observations and investigating the nature of gravity.

Representative papers:

- [Strong-field effects on emission-line profiles from Kerr black holes and warped disks (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/744/2/186)
- [Compact-binary waveforms in Brans-Dicke theory in an expanding Universe (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.108.024006)
